Waterloo Engineering Competition!

Overview

The Waterloo Engineering Competition (WEC) is an annual event that brings together engineering students from various faculties to showcase their skills, creativity, and problem-solving abilities. The competition typically involves a series of challenges that test participants technical knowledge, teamwork, and innovation. My teammates and I signed up for the junior design competition, in which we are given a complex engineering problem and are required to design and build a working prototype. We then present our solution and test our prototype in front of a panel of judges.

The Problem

This year's challenge was to design a floating mechanical rescue vehicle aimed at transporting aid to various communities affected by heavy flooding, within the constraint of $2000. Additional challenges were that the two communities were of different elevations, and there were some obstacles in the way to clearly dictate the path we needed to take. By the end of the 8 hours, we needed to have a working prototype and presentation for the judges showing our process such as idea sketches, materials list, FBD of final sketch, etc.

The Guidelines

Design Requirements:

  • A maximum size of 13 cm x 13 cm
  • The vehicle must be able to float and navigate through water.
  • Mechanical power system.
  • Controlled delivery at each station.
  • The vehicle must be constructed using materials within the given budget.

Environmental Constraints:

  • Variable water currents and floating debris.
  • Different elevations between the two communities.
  • Restricted width channels.

Human Intervention:

  • Initial launch of the vehicle.
  • Adjust vehicle at mid-point. You may re-orient/turn vehicle for a max of 5 seconds.
  • Point penalty for any other additional contact.
  • Completion of entire run with no post-launch contact will result in 30 bonus points.

Our Solution

To address the challenge, our team developed a boat that would be propelled by two different mechanisms. The first will be a balloon that will be attached to a straw, utilising the wind as an initial force to get the boat moving. The second mechanism is an elastic band/wheel/popsicle stick that will replicate boat motors. On the top, there is an elevated base with ramps on the sides for which the aid can fall off of.

Our main inspiration for the power system

The Process

After getting our challenge, my 3 teammates and I kicked off the event by taking a look at the "environment" our boat was to go through and taking measurements of that. Measurements included the path length and elevation of the two communities. We then took a look at our available materials and started sketching out ideas. We spent the next 8 hours implementing our designs, revising and iterating through our ideas. Tested on water to ensure that it floated and moved, but ultimately were unable to implement the second part of the mechanical propulsion system that we had in mind. Didn't end up winning the competition, but the experience gained was indispensable.
